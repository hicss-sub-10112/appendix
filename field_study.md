## Online Feedback Workshops - Thematic Analysis

We applied thematic analysis to gather an initial set of 193 codes. The codes were filtered for relevancy. Afterwards, 16 themes were identified from the 67 remaining codes. This process was applied independently by three annotators to each workshop at first, and finally themes were merged together after discussing and resolving discrepencies. Overall, we identified three major themes: *factors* that determine choice of interaction modality, *issues* that came up during use, and *feature* enhancements that users wished for.

We identified three major *factors* that determined participants' choice of modality: situation, task, and personality. Participants reported that they preferred voice interaction in on-the-go-situations, where their hands were occupied or their attention was peripheral.

> *"I can do it while walking, I can do it in the subway."* (translated from German)

While the touch interaction largely required a direct visual feedback loop during use, the voice interaction only required a quick glance to verify inputs. Conversely, some participants stated that they leaned towards visual interaction while they were in their workplace, especially when using a laptop. Environmental noise was also a factor. Participants stated that voice interaction was not preferred in situations with high background since the system failed to understand them. Also, in shared office spaces with low background noise, participants did not want to disturb other employees with voice interactions.

> *"But especially when I'm sitting here in the office, it depends a bit on the situation. Because I don't want to shout through the office in the morning that I came in at such and such a time and then I want to leave."* (translated from German)

The second major factor was based on the tasks nature. Some participants stated that touch input was sometimes inaccurate or required tedious pixel-perfect inputs. Many participants found that it was easier to use voice or text to input these exact values.

> *"These 5-minute steps with my finger were a bit tricky for me. I don't have the biggest display and voice input was quite convenient"* (translated from German)

On the contrary, for even values around 15 or 5 minute steps, touch inputs were often easier since the sliders snapped when used at high dragging speed.

> *"For me, it's so quick with my finger that I didn't try any further"* (translated from German)

Those who favored touch input appreciated it especially for quick tasks. On the other hand, some participants preferred voice interaction, finding it more convenient and efficient since many tasks could be realized in one interaction.

Finally, participants also reported personal preferences for voice or touch interaction, influenced by their habits and comfort levels with technology.

> *"I'm a voice input kind of person. I also use it at home for setting alarms, for Google searches, for lots of things. And yes, I find it really practical that you don't have to look at it."* (translated from German)

> *"But I'm probably used to writing that now anyway. Now, for example, on WhatsApp with one thumb. That's why I've always stuck to this sliding method."* (translated from German)

Participants who encountered *issues* with voice input early often abandoned the input modality immediately due to the lower transparency of system capabilities, which made it difficult for users to assess and correct errors. In contrast, errors in touch input were more easily identified and rectified, making users more willing to continue using it despite occasional errors. Interestingly, participants were more likely to blame the system for issues with voice interactions, whereas they sought issues for touch interactions on their side, holding their own devices or input behavior accountable.

Specifically, some participants reported that their attempts at using voice commands failed to transmit correctly or resulted in unexpected outcomes, such as the generation of incorrect time blocks. However, we found that without our intervention, these participants were often helped or encouraged to try again by other participants during the feedback sessions. In general, many issues that participants encountered – whether with voice or touch interaction – were resolved by tips from other participants.

During the feedback sessions, participants proposed a variety of *feature* enhancements, which revealed interesting differences based on the interaction modality. For conversational interactions, participants tended to suggest features that involved more abstract reasoning and automation. Examples include the ability to apply one day’s schedule to another or automatically writing absence notes based on their input. Conversely, the feature suggestions related to touch interaction were primarily focused on improving ease of use and streamlining basic functions. Participants suggested quality-of-life improvements such as the ability to swipe between days instead of opening the calendar view, or implementing more precise scheduling tools, like minute-exact adjustments. These suggestions highlight participants' expectations for conversational interactions to handle more complex, higher-level tasks than touch interactions.

### Details about the 16 Themes
#### Factors Influencing Modality Choice

These themes explore the reasons behind a user's choice between voice and touch interaction.

* **Dragging or Speaking? (1 Code)**
    The central conflict of choosing between the two main input methods.

* **Situational Flexibility (9 Codes)**
    Users adapted their interaction style based on their environment, such as being on the move versus in a quiet office.

* **Task Flexibility (3 Codes)**
    The choice of input method often depended on the task itself; quick, broad entries were suited for touch, while voice was preferred for precise or complex scheduling.

* **Personality-Based Differences (8 Codes)**
    Individual habits and technological comfort levels played a significant role, with some users identifying as "voice-input people" while others stuck to manual methods.

#### Issues Encountered

This section covers the challenges and difficulties participants faced while using the app.

* **Voice Interaction Issues (5 Codes)**
    General problems that arose when users attempted to use voice commands.

* **LLM Issues with Time Scheduling (1 Code)**
    Specific instances of the language model misinterpreting commands related to time-blocking.

* **Voice Interaction Not Following Instructions Correctly (3 Codes)**
    Cases where the system failed to accurately execute spoken commands.

* **Precision Issues with Dragging (4 Codes)**
    Difficulties with the touch sliders, particularly in making fine, minute-level adjustments.

* **Accuracy Issues with Touch Input (2 Codes)**
    General inaccuracies and errors experienced when using the touch interface.

#### Feature Enhancements & Suggestions

This category contains the new features and improvements that users requested.

* **Integrate Language Interaction with Absence Planning (6 Codes)**
    A desire for voice commands to handle more complex tasks, such as booking vacations or sick days.

* **Swipe Between Adjacent Days (1 Code)**
    A user-friendly suggestion to allow for easy navigation between days by swiping, as an alternative to using the calendar view.

* **Better Feedback for Voice Input (5 Codes)**
    A request for the system to provide clearer confirmation and feedback after a voice command has been issued.

* **Apply Times to/from Other Days (3 Codes)**
    The suggestion to add a feature that allows for copying a day's schedule to another day.

#### General Feedback and Usage Insights

These themes capture broader observations about the user experience.

* **Positive Feedback (10 Codes)**
    General positive remarks regarding the app's functionality and overall usability.

* **App is Faster than Normal Time Tracking (5 Codes)**
    Specific praise for the app's efficiency in comparison to other time-tracking methods.

* **Usage of the Voice Interaction (1 Code)**
    General comments and observations related to how and when the voice feature was utilized.

## Updates During the Study

| Week | Update | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Granular Time Entries | Participants encountered problems schedule their time accurately with 5-minute steps. To fix this, we implemented minute-level scheduling at variable precision based on slider dragging speed to mitigate precision issues related to the tedious pixel-perfect inputs. |
| | Haptic Slider Improvements | Participants encountered issues when using sliders on some devices, especially when initially grabbing the sliders. To fix this, we improved slider responsiveness by increasing the draggable area, and implemented haptic feedback. |
| | Time Block Management | Some participants reported difficulties with removing time blocks from their schedule. We introduced functionality for deleting time blocks by dragging their start below their end, and vice versa. |
| **2** | Multi-Day Absences | Added support for booking multi-day vacation and home office requests. This update also introduced manager approval features for these requests, streamlining the approval process and aligning the application with common organizational practices for leave management. |
| | Time Block Requirements | Implemented new rules for time blocks and breaks, setting a maximum duration of 6 hours before a break is required and maintaining the policy that breaks shorter than 15 minutes are not counted. |
| **3** | Date Navigation Enhancement | As suggested in the feedback workshops, we added swipe functionality quickly switch between days by swiping from the sides of the screen. We implemented haptic and visual feedback to indicate when the user can let go. |
| | Performance Optimization | We conducted improvements to the API to enhance performance, reducing loading times and improve the overall responsiveness of the application. |
| | Haptic Feedback Integration | Integrated haptic feedback for interactive elements, such as buttons, to provide users with tactile confirmation of their actions. |
| **4** | Calendar View Update | Enhanced the calendar view to show office days, home office days, vacation, and sick days for better time management visibility. |
